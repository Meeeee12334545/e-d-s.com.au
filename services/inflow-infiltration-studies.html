Stormwater and groundwater in the sewer is one of the most expensive problems a council or utility can have, and one of the hardest to see. Finding it is what we do best.

Industry data suggests inflow and infiltration (I&I) can make up half the flow in an older network during a storm. Left alone, it hides the system’s true capacity and muddies the hydraulic model. Pipes and plants end up too big or too small.

We measure where the water gets in and how much, so your money goes to the repairs that make the biggest difference.

Why I&I matters

OperationalOverloaded networks in wet weather, bypass pumping, emergency response, and the cost of pumping and treating water that should never have reached the sewer.
EnvironmentalA greater risk of overflows in big storms, assets that wear out faster, and more energy and emissions spent on water that shouldn’t be there.
FinancialAvoidable treatment costs, and money spent on bigger pipes and plants when cutting I&I could delay the upgrade.
PlanningFlow records that hide the true daily pattern, making models and forecasts harder to trust.

Monitoring Done Properly

  • High-resolution logging, typically every 1 to 5 minutes, to capture storms and the daily pattern
  • Inspections of hydraulics, access and the risk of rag or debris before a site is chosen
  • Intrinsically safe equipment for live networks, certified under ATEX and IECEx
  • Rain gauges in the catchment, so each wet weather response is tied to the rain that caused it
  • QA/QC throughout: data validation, calibration checks and remote telemetry

From Data to Action

In our experience, and across the water industry, about 20% of a sewer network typically carries 80% of its I&I. Finding that 20% is what makes a program pay back.

  • Targeted repairs

    The data finds the worst sections for relining, grouting and manhole sealing, so you don’t have to rehabilitate everything.

  • Capital works planning

    I&I analysis built into long-term asset plans, reducing the need for costly upgrades.

  • Performance measurement

    Monitoring after the works shows whether each repair delivered.

Common Questions

What is the difference between inflow and infiltration?

Inflow is stormwater that enters directly, through cross connections, unapproved private connections and faulty manholes. It arrives quickly during a storm. Infiltration is groundwater that seeps in through cracks, joints and faulty connections. It builds slowly and lingers.

How are the two told apart?

By reading flow against rainfall. Gauges in the catchment and historical climate data separate the fast response of inflow from the slow, groundwater-driven response of infiltration.

How many flow meters does a study need?

It depends on the size of the network and the question being asked. We choose sites by their hydraulics, after inspecting them. One regional council’s study, ahead of a treatment plant upgrade, used 20 short-term flow meters.

What do we receive at the end?

Validated data, and a report that ranks subcatchments by how much they contribute, with a prioritised repair plan. Monitoring after the works shows whether they cut I&I.
